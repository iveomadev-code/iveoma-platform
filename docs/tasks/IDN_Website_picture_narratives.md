## Building Legacies: The Sir & Lady Nwani Chuku Hall

*The Iveoma Development Network delivers on its promise to Holy Rosary Secondary School; dedicating a new assembly hall and consecrating a future for generations of students.*

> "Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the world — and a worthy hall is where that power begins to gather."

In 2021, the Iveoma Development Network (IDN) achieved a landmark milestone in its ongoing commitment to community upliftment: the completion, dedication, and consecration of a brand-new assembly hall at Holy Rosary Secondary School, proudly named the Sir & Lady Nwani Chuku Hall in honour of a family whose generosity made it all possible.

For years, Holy Rosary Secondary School had operated without an adequate central hall — a gathering space where students could assemble, celebrate achievements, host debates, and forge the communal bonds that make school life meaningful beyond the classroom. The IDN, driven by a mandate to invest in the foundational infrastructure of Iveoma communities, identified this gap and responded decisively.

The hall was formally consecrated by Bishop Emeritus Michael Okoro, in a ceremony attended by students, staff, community leaders, and well-wishers. The blessing transformed the building from infrastructure into a living symbol of faith, sacrifice, and collective ambition — a place made holy not just by prayer but by the intent to educate.

## Crisis Response: 2020 — When the World Stopped, Iveoma Stepped Forward

*As COVID-19 threatened lives and livelihoods across Nigeria, the Iveoma Development Network mobilised quickly — distributing essential protective supplies and providing emergency cash relief to the most vulnerable in Ebonyi State.*

In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic triggered unprecedented disruption across the world and Ebonyi State was no exception. Government-mandated sit-at-home orders, while necessary to curb the spread of the virus, brought daily economic life to a halt. For countless residents — traders, artisans, subsistence earners — the absence of income was not an inconvenience; it was a survival crisis.

The Iveoma Development Network, true to its founding mission of community-centred development, refused to sit on the sidelines. The Network mounted a targeted relief effort: procuring and distributing critical health supplies across public institutions while extending direct financial assistance to those who had been left most economically exposed.

In a period of widespread uncertainty, the IDN took concrete, coordinated action across multiple fronts:

### Hand Wash Basins
Installed hand-washing facilities at schools and churches — key public gathering points — to promote basic hygiene as the first line of defence against transmission.

### Hand Sanitizers
Distributed hand sanitizers to institutions across Ebonyi State, ensuring that even communities without running water had access to hygienic protection.

### Face Masks
Supplied face masks to protect students, church members, and the general public in communities where access to PPE was severely limited.

### Emergency Cash Relief
Provided direct cash support to indigent persons in Ebonyi State whose livelihoods had been disrupted by the government-sanctioned sit-at-home period.

By targeting schools and churches — the two most attended communal spaces in the community — the Network maximised the reach and visibility of hygiene protocols. These were spaces where trust already existed, making public health messaging far more likely to be absorbed and practised.

> "A network that only shows up in good times is not truly a network. Iveoma's response in 2020 was a demonstration of solidarity — not charity, but the recognition that the community's pain belongs to all of us."

As the world looks back on those difficult months, the Network's quiet, determined response in Ebonyi State stands as evidence that when people are organised around shared values, no crisis is too large to face together.

## Walking Together, Building Tomorrow — The Annual Health Walk

Since 2021, the Iveoma Development Network's Annual Health Walk has gathered the community in motion and purpose. Members at home and in the diaspora have laced up their shoes and walked together. The IDN Annual Health Walk is both a wellness event and a declaration: that the community moves as one, that health is a collective value, and that showing up for one another is the foundation of everything the Network builds.

The walk has grown into one of the Network's most visible and beloved traditions — a moment when development work pauses for participation, and when the community's bonds are renewed in the most human way possible: side by side, in motion.

At the 5th edition of the Annual Health Walk, the Iveoma Development Network broke ground on its most ambitious youth-facing project to date — a dedicated digital and learning hub for adolescents and young people, named in honour of Sir Nwani and Lady Akanele Chuku.

The Learning and Development Centre is designed around the needs of today's young people, combining digital skills, reading culture, and social development under one roof:

### Digital Skills Hub
A dedicated space for adolescents to develop practical digital competencies: computing, internet literacy, and emerging technology skills.

### Reading & Learning Centre
A curated environment designed to cultivate the reading habit and support self-directed learning among young people in the community.

### Social Activities Hub
A gathering space for youth-led programmes, mentorship sessions, and the kind of peer connection that shapes character and community.

### Youth Development Focus
The centre is purpose-built for adolescents and young people, filling a gap that no other facility in the community currently addresses.

## A New Examination Hall for Isi Okposi Girls

*The Iveoma Development Network renovates and fully equips a dedicated examination hall at Isi Okposi Girls Secondary School — a state government institution — handing a public school the facilities its students always deserved.*

Think about the last time you sat an important exam. Now imagine doing it in a room that was cramped, poorly lit, and visibly neglected — a room that, before you wrote a single word, already told you something about how much the system valued you.

That was the reality for students at Isi Okposi Girls Secondary School. Not because they lacked ability or ambition, but simply because the infrastructure around them had not kept pace with their potential. The Iveoma Development Network saw that, and in 2016/2017, did something about it.

The Network renovated and fully equipped a dedicated examination hall at the school and commissioned it in a ceremony attended by students, staff, and community members. It was a straightforward act with a profound message: that these girls, in a public school, in a small community in Ebonyi State, are worth investing in.

> *Every girl who walks into that hall to write her exams now does so in a space that was built with her in mind. That is not a small thing.*
